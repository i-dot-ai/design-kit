How to get good answers

How to write questions that get precise, useful answers from Parlex.

Key principles

Be specific about timeframes

Use "since the election", "in the last 3 months", or specific dates. If you don't give a timeframe, Parlex uses the current parliamentary period.

Always verify information

Check AI responses against official Hansard records and parliamentary sources before using them in briefings or reports. Large language models are not perfect. They can make mistakes and can hallucinate.

Ask for specific outputs

Ask for quotes, summaries, member profiles or thematic analysis to get structured results. Give a word count or format (for example "300 words of prose" or "short bullets") to control the length.

Paste Hansard links directly

You can paste a Hansard link into your question and ask Parlex to summarise or analyse the debate. You do not need to describe the debate. The link is enough.

Be specific about committees

When asking about committee evidence sessions, include the committee name, the session title and the date. For example: "What did Samir Haddad say at the Work and Pensions Committee session on Disability Employment, 4 February 2026?"

Task, source and time

Build your question from 3 parts to get precise results.

1. Task

Say exactly what you want Parlex to do, as if you were asking a junior colleague. Be specific about the type of analysis, comparison or information you need.

Do
  • Say what type of output you want: "compare", "find", "summarise", "identify"
  • Ask for structured formats: "show quotes", "list members", "group by party"
  • Ask for analysis: "identify themes", "compare positions", "track how views changed"
  • Ask for a format: "Generate a bullet point summary of PMQs from Wednesday 5 November"
Don't
  • Use vague requests like "tell me about" or "what's happening"
  • Ask for predictions or future outcomes
  • Ask for information outside parliamentary data (for example social media or news articles)
  • Ask open-ended questions without saying what you want back (for example "tell me about health")

2. Source

Say which parliamentary sources or data types to search. Filter by members, parties, committees or departments.

Do
  • Name data types: "debates", "parliamentary questions", "committee proceedings"
  • Filter by members: "Conservative MPs", "shadow ministers", "Northern MPs"
  • Name specific bodies: "Treasury Committee", "Department for Education"
  • Exclude groups when relevant: "excluding government ministers"
  • Be specific: "Who spoke at Wednesday's debate titled 'Drug-related Deaths'?"
Don't
  • Assume Parlex searches all sources automatically. Be explicit
  • Ask for non-parliamentary sources (social media, press releases, external reports)
  • Assume Parlex knows anything from non-parliamentary sources

3. Time

Set the timeframe for your research. Parlex uses the current parliamentary period unless you say otherwise.

Do
  • Use relative timeframes: "since the election", "in the last 3 months"
  • Give exact dates: "between January and March 2024"
  • Use parliamentary periods: "current session", "since the start of this Parliament"
  • Ask for a number of recent items: "the 10 most recent debates"
Don't
  • Use vague time references like "recently" or "lately" without context
  • Ask for data from today (Parlex includes data up to yesterday)
  • Assume Parlex knows the timeframe you mean. Always say

When an answer looks wrong

If Parlex doesn't find what you expected, open Research steps above the answer. It shows how Parlex understood your question and what it actually searched for.

Research plan

The plan lists the steps Parlex intended to take. Check that it matches what you asked.

Example:

Research plan: Search for the most recent parliamentary written questions where the Department for Transport is the answering body. Collect and display a list of these recent questions.

Steps

Each step shows what Parlex actually searched and how many records it found:

  • the source, for example written questions or Commons debates
  • the search terms
  • the date range
  • any filters, for example the answering department or House

Compare the two

If the plan says Parlex will search the last year, but the step shows it searched the last month, something went wrong. Ask again with a clearer task, source or time. Then tell us using Not helpful, so we can fix it.

Help us improve Parlex

Your feedback shows us what works and what needs to improve. Every answer has Helpful and Not helpful buttons at the bottom.

Helpful and Not helpful

Select Helpful (thumbs up) when Parlex gives a useful answer. Select Not helpful (thumbs down) when the answer isn't what you wanted or contains errors.

Add a comment

After either button, you can add a comment. Comments on Not helpful answers tell us exactly what went wrong. Comments on Helpful answers tell us what to keep.

Example comments:

  • "Searched for the wrong time period"
  • "Missing key MPs who should have been included"
  • "Results don't match what I asked for"

How we use your feedback

We use ratings and comments in our evaluation process to find patterns and improve Parlex. Your feedback goes directly into the next round of improvements.

About

About Parlex

What Parlex uses, what it cannot do, and how to get help.

Overview

Parlex is an AI parliamentary research tool for UK civil servants. It searches and analyses parliamentary data, including debates, member contributions and parliamentary questions.

Hallucinations

Always check information against the original sources before you use it.

Like all tools that use large language models (LLMs), Parlex can hallucinate. This means it can make up information that is not in the original data. We work to reduce this, and we are building evaluations to measure how often it happens. Asking for citations and references helps, but it does not remove the risk.

Usage and limitations

Parlex is a tool for parliamentary research, like an advanced search engine for parliamentary data. It is not designed to predict the outcome of bills, policies or elections.

The quality of results depends on data quality, model accuracy and how precise your question is. Parlex may miss nuances in debate, such as tone and wider political context.

Parlex only uses data from Parliament: Hansard contributions, parliamentary questions, debates, committee proceedings and member information. It does not have access to social media or other external sources.

Parliamentary data is updated daily. Parlex includes data up to and including yesterday. It does not have today's proceedings, because they are still being processed.

What an MP or Peer said in Parliament may not show their views today. Members can change their views, may be whipped to follow the party line, could say different things on social media, and their role may affect what they can say or how they say it.

Never use generated summaries as definitive sources. Check them against the original contributions. Use Parlex as a starting point, as you would a Google search.

Feedback and support

Parlex is in development, and we welcome your feedback. Email parlex@cabinetoffice.gov.uk to send feedback or report a problem.

Access to Parlex

Parlex is available to all UK civil servants and parliamentary staff. We may need to approve your organisation if it is not already on the approved list. If you cannot access Parlex, email parlex@cabinetoffice.gov.uk.

Powered by Parliament

Thank you to the Parliamentary Digital Service team for the APIs that power both Parlex and the Parliament MCP.
