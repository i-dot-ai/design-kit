title: Chart colours
description: The colour palette for data visualisations, and how to use it.
---

Use the categorical colours by default. They are the most different from each
other, so they make categories easiest to tell apart.

Only use a sequential or divergent scale when your data has an order, such as
low to high, or negative to positive.

These colours follow the [GOV.UK chart palette](https://brand.design-system.service.gov.uk/colour/charts/).

## Do not rely on colour alone

Some people cannot tell colours apart, and charts are often printed in black and
white. Always give each category another way to identify it:

- a clear label or legend
- a different line style (solid, dashed or dotted) on line, area and radar charts
- the data in a table

Make sure colours contrast with the background and with any text on top of them.

## Categorical colours

Use these to tell categories apart, such as the lines on a line chart or the
slices of a pie chart.

The [chart component](/components/chart) uses them in this order when you do not
set a colour. Use them in the same order if you set colours yourself.

| Order | Colour | Name | Chart name | Hex |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | <Swatch colour="#1d70b8" /> | Primary blue | `blue` | #1D70B8 |
| 2 | <Swatch colour="#0f385c" /> | Blue shade 50% | `blue-shade-50` | #0F385C |
| 3 | <Swatch colour="#ca357c" /> | Primary magenta | `magenta` | #CA357C |
| 4 | <Swatch colour="#7f65b7" /> | Purple tint 25% | `purple-tint-25` | #7F65B7 |
| 5 | <Swatch colour="#50a1a5" /> | Teal tint 25% | `teal-tint-25` | #50A1A5 |
| 6 | <Swatch colour="#f7996a" /> | Orange tint 25% | `orange-tint-25` | #F7996A |

If you only have one category, use primary blue.

Use no more than 5 colours in one chart. If you have more categories, group the
smaller ones together or split the chart into several charts.

## Sequential and divergent scales

Use a scale when colour shows a value, not a category. For example, a heatmap or
a rating from "very poor" to "very good".

- **Sequential**: use one colour, from light for low values to dark for high
  values. For example, blue tint 80% up to blue shade 50%.
- **Divergent**: use two colours that meet at a neutral middle. For example,
  red for negative, black tint 80% for neutral and blue for positive.

Use no more than 2 scales in one chart.
